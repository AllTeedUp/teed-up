What All Teed Up Does

If you are the booking creator, share a screenshot of your golf booking with All Teed Up and it goes into every player's calendar as an invite.

Everyone else on the booking does nothing. They get a calendar invite and accept or decline it, and their answer comes back to the creator.

Three things that gets you:

  • Eliminate or at least reduce no-show fines. The round is in everyone's diary, with reminders — not just the booker's.
  • Remember who you are playing with. The invite names everyone playing.
  • No double booking. The app checks your calendar before it saves and tells you if something else is already at that tee time.

It is for everyone on the booking. Whoever made it adds it, everyone else accepts the invite. Any round booked with All Teed Up that you are on appears in the app, with your own answer against each.

When you add a booking, the app asks what it is first, so it reads the screenshot the right way:

  • A booking I just made — the other players get a calendar invite.
  • A booking just for me — recorded in your calendar, nobody invited.
  • A tournament I'm playing in — the club draws the field, so no invites are sent.

Everything runs on your phone. No accounts, no sign-up, and nothing leaves the device except the calendar invite itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Someone declined — what happens?

You are told, without having to check. The app then suggests who to ask instead, taken from the people you play with most across the rounds you have organised. It cannot tell you who is free — nothing on your phone knows that — so check with them first.

Swap your replacement in and the calendar does the rest: the player who dropped out comes off the invite, the new one gets theirs, and everyone still playing gets an updated invite saying what changed, so nobody is left wondering why it reappeared.

My booking has a slot I haven't filled — can I still add it?

Yes. If the booking is for four and only three are named, the empty seat shows as TBC on the round and as an open slot on the calendar invite. Add the fourth player when you find them and they get an invitation then.

Can I let friends and family know I'm playing?

Yes, if you made the booking. Add friends or family once under Friends & Family in Settings, then tick whoever you want when you add a booking. They get the round for information, so somebody at home knows which course you are at and when you are due back.

They are never counted as players. They cannot be mistaken for one when the round is read back, and they are not asked to accept anything. You can have them ticked by default on every new booking if you would rather not think about it each time.

It is only available to the person who made the booking. On a round somebody else added you to, there is nothing to tick — you cannot add your friends and family to their round.

What else does it do that I would otherwise do by hand?

  • Finds the email addresses. Player names on the booking are matched against your own contacts, on the phone, so the invite reaches people rather than sitting there unaddressed.
  • Puts the course in as the location, so a maps app can navigate to it, and carries the booking reference with the round.
  • Reminders 12 hours and 1 hour before, on by default and adjustable in Settings.
  • Tells you when somebody answers — declines arrive as a notification and sit in Alerts.
  • Keeps played rounds under Past rounds rather than deleting them.
  • Takes a shared screenshot straight from Photos or WhatsApp, and asks what it is before it reads it.
  • Falls back to manual entry if a screenshot cannot be read, instead of leaving you stuck.

Does this actually stop no-show fines?

It removes the commonest reason people miss a tee time: it was only ever in the organiser's diary. With the round in everyone's calendar, named, and with reminders 12 hours and 1 hour before, far fewer people forget.

Is it only for multiplayer bookings?

That is what it was designed for. The problem All Teed Up solves is managing multi-player bookings: Viya and most booking systems already send the booker a calendar invite, so the gap is everyone else on the booking.

Managing single-player bookings and tournament entries was added by request during testing. Both work, and both are on the home screen — they are simply not the reason the app exists.

Do the other players need the app?

No. Only the booking creator adds the booking. Everyone else gets an ordinary calendar invite and accepts or declines it, and their answer comes back to the creator.

If they do have it, every round anyone books them into with All Teed Up appears automatically — nothing to forward or type in — in one list, with their own answer against each and a badge on any they have not answered yet.

Why does the app need Camera / Photos access?

To read the screenshot of your booking confirmation. Text recognition runs entirely on your phone — the image is never sent anywhere.

Why does the app need Contacts access?

To match player names on your booking to their email addresses, so calendar invites can be addressed correctly. This lookup happens entirely on-device.

Why does the app need Calendar access?

To create the round as an event in your device's own calendar and to read replies back. It works with any account your phone publishes — iCloud, Google, Outlook, Exchange or Yahoo — on both iPhone and Android. Syncing is done by your phone, not by us.

Why does the app need Notification access?

To tell you when a player declines a round you have organised. Notifications are generated on your device from your own calendar — nothing is sent from a server, and no push identifier is created.

It didn't pick up the right details — what do I do?

Booking screenshots come in a lot of formats. If a course, time or player is missed or wrong, correct it on the review screen before the round is saved — nothing is created until you confirm. If the App thinks you picked the wrong option — a tournament read as an ordinary booking, or the reverse — it says so on that screen and you can go back and choose again. And if it could not read the screenshot at all, it takes you to manual entry rather than saving a half-read round.

My calendar isn't in the list — where is it?

Almost always because the calendar has not been published to your phone, so no app can see it — not All Teed Up, not your phone's own calendar app. Outlook is the usual case: it keeps its calendar inside its own app until you switch sync on.

You do not need to know how. In the calendar list, tap Google, Outlook, Work / Exchange or Yahoo and the App opens the right screen for you. If your phone refuses, the steps are shown instead:

  • Android — in Outlook, tap your profile picture, then the gear icon, then your account under Mail Accounts, and turn on Sync calendars.
  • iPhone — Outlook on iOS keeps its calendar to itself. Add the account to iOS Calendar instead: Settings › Apps › Calendar › Calendar Accounts › Add Account › Outlook.com (or Exchange for a work account), and turn Calendars on.

Then come back and tap Refresh list. If it still is not there, tap Still not showing? — that shows exactly what your phone reported, and you can copy it into an email to us.

Which calendar should I choose?

One that belongs to a real account — Google, Outlook, Exchange, iCloud or Yahoo. The app picks one for you and prefers an account that can actually deliver invitations. A calendar that lives only on the phone (often called "On My iPhone", "Phone", "Local", or "Birthdays") cannot send an invite or receive a reply. On iPhone it is worse than that: iOS refuses to save a booking for other players to one at all, rather than saving it without the invitation. The app will not choose such a calendar unless it is the only one you have, and it tells you plainly if you pick one.

If you would rather not take our word for which calendars work, don't. In Settings, under your calendar, tap Check this calendar can invite people. It puts a temporary entry in that calendar with your own address on it, reads it back to see whether the invitation survived, removes it again, and tells you what happened. Nobody else is ever put on that entry.

I added a tournament and it shows no other players — is that wrong?

No, that is correct. A club competition is an entry for you alone: the club draws the field and publishes it closer to the day, so there is nobody to invite yet. Your entry shows the venue, the format and its status, and you are listed as the player. When the draw comes out, share it with the app and add your playing partners then.

How do I see whether I've accepted a round someone added me to?

Tap Bookings I've been added to on the home screen. Each one shows who added you and whether you have accepted, declined or not yet answered — and the number beside it is how many are still waiting on you. To answer, open the invitation in your calendar or email; the app reads your reply back from the calendar.

The booking changed — how do I update it?

Open the round and tap Amend Booking, then share the new confirmation with the app. The app shows you exactly what changed — who was added and who was removed — before anything is saved. Players who were already on the round get an updated invitation that says what changed, rather than a silent duplicate.

Will I end up with the same round twice?

The app checks before it saves. If a round at that course and time already exists it offers to update it instead of adding a second one, and if something unrelated is already in your calendar at that time it tells you and lets you decide. Nothing is created or replaced without you confirming.

I shared a screenshot into the app — will it know what it is?

It asks. Sharing a screenshot from Photos, WhatsApp or anywhere else opens the app and asks what you have got, exactly as the home screen does, before it reads anything. That matters most for tournaments: read as an ordinary booking, a competition entry would list the form's own labels as players.

What does All Teed Up cost?

AED 99 a year, billed through the app Store or Google Play. Manage or cancel it in your store account settings at any time — cancelling stops future renewals and you keep access until the current period ends. If the subscription lapses you can still see rounds you have already organised; adding new ones needs an active subscription.

How do I remove my data?

Uninstalling the app deletes everything stored on your device. Calendar events already created remain in your device calendar unless you delete them separately, since they're managed by your calendar provider, not the app.
